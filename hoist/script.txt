Ten strong men couldn't lift this horse. One skinny old man did. With one hand. In the kingdom of Hoistwell, the king wants his favorite horse on top of the castle wall. For a royal portrait. Ten builders grab the rope. They pull. They sweat. They fly off the ground. The horse doesn't move. Then the old rope maker climbs up with a bag of wooden wheels. He hangs one wheel on the wall. One on the horse. Then another. And another. The rope goes up, down, up, down. He pulls gently… and the horse rises. Like an elevator. Still eating hay. Here's why. Each time the rope loops around another wheel, the weight gets shared by one more piece of rope. Four ropes holding the horse means each one carries just a quarter. You pull four times more rope… but with a quarter of the effort. That's a block and tackle. The portrait was perfect. The horse refused to come down.
