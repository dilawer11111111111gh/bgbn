The fastest train in Clatterbrook could outrun a horse.
It could not survive one single curve.
The town tinkerer built it with perfect wheels. Perfectly flat.
On the straight track… beautiful.
Then came the first curve.
The wheels screeched. Sparks. Smoke.
Curve two… the train jumped the rails and parked inside the saloon.
Curve three… it took the bank. With the banker still inside.
So the tinkerer did something nobody understood.
He shaved every wheel into a cone. Fat on the inside. Thin on the outside.
The whole town laughed…
until the train took every curve like butter.
You see, on a curve, the outer rail is longer than the inner one.
So the train slides outward.
Now the outer wheel rolls on its fat part, and the inner wheel on its thin part.
A fatter wheel covers more ground in one turn.
The train steers itself. No steering wheel. Just cones.
And that's how trains learned to turn.
Every train still rides on cones today.
And the banker? He still takes the stairs.
